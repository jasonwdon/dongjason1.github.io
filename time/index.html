Time is Made Up

A Linguistic Perspective

by Jason

A lightning talk from Etsy's Eng Week, 2020

This talk looks at time from a linguistic perspective. The idea came, as usual, from going down a rabbit hole during an important meeting.

“I look forward to our meeting”

2

The rabbit hole starts here. We say we look forward to something in the future, as if the event were physically in front of us. Time is completely abstract. Nobody can point in a direction and say, that's where the future is.

English gives time a spatial dimension anyway, to make it easier to understand. The future is in front of us and the past is behind us. That makes sense, right?

“I look forward to our meeting”

“Can we push our meeting back?”

3

Then we ask to push a meeting back, and we mean move it further into the future. Push it… back… to the future?

We can actually explain this. If an object is in front of us and we want it to back up, we push it backwards. Time is still a dimension, future events are objects in front of us, and pushing them back sends them further into the future. So what do we say when we want a meeting earlier?

“I look forward to our meeting”

“Can we push our meeting back?”

“Let's move it up instead”

4

We move time… up? This is still consistent with the object metaphor. Things close to us are up close, and we ask people to bring it up to us. Directionally we're all over the place, but technically we're consistent.

All of this is linguistics, the scientific study of language. Applied to computer science, it gives us tools like spelling correction and machine translation. But natural language processing has been moving away from classical linguistics and toward statistical machine learning for a long time now.

Anytime a linguist leaves the group the recognition rate goes up

— Frederick Jelinek

5

A speech recognition researcher is often quoted saying this. I prefer a quote from my favorite linguist.

“Why waste time say lot word when few word do trick?”

— Kevin Malone

6

Okay, but back to time.

Time is a dimension

7

As we've seen, time is a dimension, and in English it's a one-dimensional line. Sometimes it runs forwards and backwards. Other times it's a timeline from left to right.

“Can we move the meeting up?”

“Time's up!”

“I could use some downtime”

8

We also use "up", like moving a meeting up or "time's up!" There's no moving meetings down or time being down, but we do have uptime and downtime.

9

In Mandarin, time is also dimensional, but it moves in the opposite direction! The day after tomorrow is hòu tiān, and "in the future" is yǐ hòu, both using the word for behind. For the past we say qián tiān and yǐ qián, using the word for front. This isn't wrong, just a different perspective. Think of time as a book: the beginning is at the front, and the end is at the back.

Time is money

10

Another common metaphor in English is time as money, a valuable commodity.

“Spend more time with your friends”

“Save some time for me!”

“Budget your time accordingly”

11

Every day we spend time, save time and budget our time, always thinking about what it's worth.

“There's no time to waste!”

“Living on borrowed time”

“I've invested a lot of time in this”

12

In darker ways, time can be wasted. It can be borrowed, which means we owe time to someone or something. Worst of all, we invest time, which suggests we're taking a risk and expecting returns. Good thing investments only go up, right?

English:
Spend time
Spanish:
Pasar el tiempoPass
Czech:
Trávit časDigest
13

The phenomenon isn't universal. In Spanish you don't spend time, you pass it. In Czech, you don't spend it, you digest it! I think it's funny to think of time as something you're continually eating. It takes enjoying your time to a whole new level.

It's not perfect, and these languages have other ways of describing time as money, but it's fun to see time through a different lens.

Time is...

the indefinite continued progress of existence and events in the past, present, and future regarded as a whole

the progression of events from the past to the present into the future

the continued sequence of existence and events that occurs in an apparently irreversible succession from the past, through the present, into the future

14

So to bring it all together, what is time, really? We have definitions, but they all use time to define time. Past, present and future do all the work, which makes them circular. To make it easier to understand, we have these metaphors. Is time money? A dimension? A digestive tract? In physics, time is relative. Relative to light? To me and you? Maybe time is just the speed of the computer that's simulating our reality.

On a more personal note, I can't help but feel the pressure not to waste time. The internet constantly tells us to pack more into our schedules. I can't tell you what time really is, but our language mirrors our perspective. What if we didn't see time as a limited resource, but as the naturally occurring phenomenon that it is?

Time is

15

We would never say we're wasting gravity, or that we've spent too much light, ya know? So smell the roses, pet the dogs. Time is made up, so we might as well enjoy it.
