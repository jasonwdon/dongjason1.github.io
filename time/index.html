Time is Made Up

A Linguistic Perspective

By Jason

A lightning talk from Etsy's Eng Week, 2021

The idea for this talk came from a classic overfixation from a bunch of linguistic nerds.

“I look forward to our meeting”

2

Someone pointed out how interesting it is that we look forward to something in the future, as if the event was physically in front of us.

For some reason, we give time a spacial direction and talk about events as objects along the dimension. The future is in front of us and the past is behind us. That makes sense, right?

“I look forward to our meeting”

“Can we push our meeting back?”

3

Then we ask to push a meeting back when we want to move it further into the future. Push it… back… to the future?

This is actually still consistent with our metaphor. If we want to move an object further away from us, we push it back. So what do we say when we want a meeting earlier?

“I look forward to our meeting”

“Can we push our meeting back?”

“Let's move it up instead”

4

Yet again, remarkably consistent. Things close to us are up close, and we ask people to bring it up to us. Directionally we're all over the place, but the metaphor holds.

Linguistics can be weird when you look too closely. Nowdays, we don't really think about these quirks when we're working on spelling correction and machine translation. For a while now, natural language processing has moved away from classical linguistics and toward statistical machine learning.

Anytime a linguist leaves the group the recognition rate goes up

— Frederick Jelinek

5

A speech recognition researcher is often quoted saying this, but I prefer a quote from my favorite linguist.

“Why waste time say lot word when few word do trick?”

— Kevin Malone

6

Okay, but back to time.

Time is a dimension

7

As we've seen, time can be treated spacially as a one dimensional line. Sometimes it runs forwards and backwards, sometimes it's a timeline running from left to right.

“Time's up!”

“I could use some downtime”

8

Sometimes, time is a vertical dimension, although we don't really move meetings down or say that time is down, but we do have uptime and downtime.

9

In Mandarin, time is also dimensional, but it moves in the opposite direction. The day after tomorrow uses the word for behind while the day before yesterday uses the word for front. We can think of this like a book where the beginning is at the front, and the end is at the back.

Time is money

10

Another common metaphor in English is time as money.

“Spend more time with your friends”

“Save some time for me!”

“Budget your time accordingly”

11

Every day we spend time, save time and budget time, always keeping track of time's inherent value.

“There's no time to waste!”

“Living on borrowed time”

“I've invested a lot of time in this”

12

Time can be wasted, borrowed, and even invested. Good thing the stock market always goes up.

English:
Spend time
Spanish:
Pasar el tiempoPass
Czech:
Trávit časDigest
13

Some languages use less stressful metaphors for time. In Spanish you don't spend time, you pass it and In Czech, you don't spend it, you digest it. For me, eating your time takes enjoying time to a whole new level.

These languages do treat time as money in other ways, but it's nice to know there are other metaphors we can use.

Time is...

the indefinite continued progress of existence and events in the past, present, and future regarded as a whole

the progression of events from the past to the present into the future

the continued sequence of existence and events that occurs in an apparently irreversible succession from the past, through the present, into the future

14

So, what is time, really? Without our metaphors, we'll find that the definitions of time are all a bit circular in that they use the concept of time to define time.

To make it easier to understand, we treat it as a spacial dimension, money, and even a digestive tract.

On a more personal note, I can't help but feel the pressure not to waste time. Subconciously, the language we use can affect our perspective and I worry that the scarcity view might be affecting my enjoyment of time. What if we didn't see it as a limited resource, but rather as the naturally occurring phenomenon that it is?

Time is

15

We would never say we're wasting gravity, or that we've spent too much light. So smell the roses, pet the dogs. Time is made up, we might as well enjoy it.
