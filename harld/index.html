harld

by jason

In 2019 I was at Case Western Reserve University taking EECS 325 (Computer Networks I, now known as CSDS 325) with Dr. Michael Rabinovich when he kindly showed us that the university’s email relay would forward anything you sent it. It didn’t check what the message said, who you were sending it to, or, most importantly, whether you were actually the person in the “From” line.

Suddenly, our friends were getting concerning emails from professors, celebrities, and even from themselves, from the future. Then someone had a better idea.

If you lived in the dorms, packages were delivered to a central hub and you’d get an email from HARLD (The Housing and Residence Life Database) telling you to pick it up. We figured that if we made the message look urgent enough, we could get someone down to the common area in record time.

We would then sit in a nearby study room and see how long it would take them to show up. Eventually, someone would come down, try to claim a package that didn’t exist, get confused, then wander over to say hi. It got to the point where the desk staff were apologizing and telling people they didn’t know why this kept happening.

The following year, the server stopped forwarding our spoofed messages. IT probably patched the email server to force some kind of auth, but for some reason they left a door open so you could still send anyone an email from themselves. There wasn’t as much we could do with this so the pranks died down, but I’ve always wondered if they ever fixed it.

side note

Avengers Endgame came out around the same time and someone emailed the whole school: Tony Stark Dies. My understanding is that there was no hack and they didn’t even use the open smtp relay. The university just left one of its school wide mailing lists with open posting permissions and it went unnoticed for months. Whoever found that list and sat on it until opening weekend has my absolute respect and admiration.
